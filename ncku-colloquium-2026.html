Simplicial Complexes

Definition (Simplicial Complex)
A simplicial complex is a pair $(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{X})$ of a set of vertices $\mathcal{V}$ and a set of simplices, $\mathcal{X} \subset 2^{\mathcal{V}}$ of finite subsets of $\mathcal{V}$, satisfying:

\[\{v\} \in \mathcal{X} \text{ for all } v \in \mathcal{V} \ (\text{called }0\text{-simplices}),\]

\[ Z \in \mathcal{X} \text{ and } Y \subset Z \implies Y \in \mathcal{X}. \]

A simplex $s\in\mathcal{X}$ is said to be a $d$-simplex ($d\geq 0$) if $\#s=d+1$.

One can associate a canonical topological space to a simplicial complex $X$, denoted by $|X|$, called its geometric realization.

  • Let $K$ be a field and $r \geq 2$.
  • The non-zero proper $K$-subspaces of $K^r$, ordered by inclusion, form the spherical Tits building $\mathcal{T}_\bullet(K^r)$.

Homotopy equivalences

  • Let $R,S$ be topological spaces. A continuous map $f: R \rightarrow S$ is a homotopy equivalence if there exists a continuous map $g: S \rightarrow R$ such that $f \circ g$ is homotopic to $1_S$ and $g \circ f$ is homotopic to $1_R$.
  • Explicitly, there exist continuous maps $H:S\times[0,1]\to S$ and $G:R\times[0,1]\to R$ with $H(s,0)=f(g(s))$, $H(s,1)=s$, and $G(r,0)=g(f(r))$, $G(r,1)=r$.
  • A topological space $X$ is contractible if it is homotopy equivalent to a point.

Induced maps on geometric realizations

  • Let $X:=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{X})$ and $Y:=(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{Y})$ be two simplicial complexes. Then $f:\mathcal{V} \rightarrow \mathcal{W}$ is said to induce a simplicial map if it sends a simplex in $\mathcal{X}$ to a simplex in $\mathcal{Y}$.
  • A simplicial map $f:X\to Y$ induces a continuous map on geometric realizations, denoted $|f|:|X|\to|Y|$.

Combinatorics to algebra

Simplicial homological complex and homology

Let $X:=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{X})$ be a simplicial complex. For each $d \geq -1$, let $C_d(X)$ be the free abelian group generated by the oriented $d$-simplices of $X$, for $d \geq 0$ and $C_{-1}(X):=\mathbb{Z}$.

The boundary map $\partial_d: C_d(X) \rightarrow C_{d-1}(X)$ is defined on a generator $\langle v_0,...,v_d\rangle$ by \[ \partial_d(\langle v_0,...,v_d\rangle) := \sum_{i=0}^d (-1)^i \langle v_0,...,\hat{v_i},...,v_d\rangle, \] where $\hat{v_i}$ indicates that the vertex $v_i$ is omitted.

$\partial_d\circ\partial_{d+1}=0$ for all $d\geq 0$.

Reduced homology

The reduced homology groups of $X$ are defined as \[ \tilde{H}_d(X,\mathbb{Z}) := \ker(\partial_d)/\text{im}(\partial_{d+1}) ; \ d \geq 0.\]

Some basic facts about homology

  • If $\lvert X \rvert$ is homotopy equivalent to $\lvert Y \rvert$, then $\tilde{H}_d(X,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \tilde{H}_d(Y,\mathbb{Z})$ for all $d \geq 0$.
  • In particular, if $\lvert X \rvert$ is contractible, then $\tilde{H}_d(X,\mathbb{Z}) = 0$ for all $d \geq 0$.

Notation/Terminology

  • Let $p>0$ be prime, $q=p^n$, and $K:=\mathbb{F}_q(t)$.
  • The place at infinity $\infty$ corresponds to the valuation $\mathbb F_q(t)^\times\to\mathbb Z$, $f/g\mapsto\deg(g)-\deg(f)$.
  • The Laurent series field in $1/t$ is \[ K_\infty:=\mathbb F_q((1/t)) =\left\{\sum_{i\ge i_0}a_i(1/t)^i\;:\;i_0\in\mathbb Z,\ a_i\in\mathbb F_q\right\}. \]
  • The power series ring in $1/t$ is \[ O_\infty:=\mathbb F_q[[1/t]] =\left\{\sum_{i\ge 0}a_i(1/t)^i\;:\;a_i\in\mathbb F_q\right\}. \]
  • The uniformizer is $\pi:=1/t$.
  • An $O_\infty$-lattice is a free $O_\infty$-submodule of $K_\infty^r$ of rank $r$. For example, $O_\infty^r$.

Bruhat–Tits Building

$\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_\bullet$ is the simplicial complex with:

  • Vertices: Homothety classes $\langle L\rangle$ of $O_\infty$-lattices $L$. That is, $\langle L\rangle=\langle L'\rangle\iff L'=\alpha L$ for some $\alpha\in K_\infty^\times$.
  • Simplices: $\{\langle L_0\rangle,...,\langle L_d\rangle\}$ forms a $d$-simplex, if after suitable rearrangement, one has $L_0 \supsetneq L_1 \supsetneq ... \supsetneq L_d \supsetneq \pi L_0$.
Simplices of maximal rank occur for $d=r-1$ and are called chambers. Each codimension-1 simplex (called a panel) adjoins $(q+1)$ chambers.
Bruhat-Tits building for $q=2$ and $r=2$

Arithmetic action

$A:=\mathbb{F}_q[t]\subset K=\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ is the ring of functions regular away from $\infty$.

Definition
$\Gamma\subset\mathrm{GL}_r(K)$ is a congruence subgroup if it contains $\Gamma(I):=\ker\bigl(\mathrm{GL}_r(A)\to\mathrm{GL}_r(A/I)\bigr)$ as a subgroup of finite index.

Here $I\subsetneq A$ is a nonzero ideal; $\Gamma(I)$ is called a principal congruence subgroup.

Example. $\mathrm{GL}_2(A)$ is a congruence subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$.

The canonical action of $\mathrm{GL}_r(K)$ on $\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_\bullet$ induces an action of $\Gamma$ on $\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_\bullet$.

The stabilizer in $\Gamma$ of a vertex v is a finite group, denoted $\Gamma_v$.

$\Gamma$-stability / instability
A simplex $s\in\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_\bullet$ is called $\Gamma$-stable if $\Gamma_s=\{1\}$; it is $\Gamma$-unstable otherwise.

Fact. If $s=\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}$, then $\displaystyle\Gamma_s=\bigcap_{0\le i\le d}\Gamma_{v_i}$.

The unstable subcomplex

Serre, 1979
Let $\Gamma \subset GL_2(K)$ be a congruence subgroup whose torsion elements have $p$-power order. Then $\lvert \mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma-un}_\bullet \rvert$ is homotopy equivalent to $\lvert \mathbb{P}^1(K) \rvert$.

Proof. Homotopy equivalence is induced by the correspondence $s \in \mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma-un}_\bullet \leftrightarrow (K^2)^{\Gamma_s} \in \mathbb{P}^1(K)$.

In particular, $\widetilde H_0(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma\text{-un}}_\bullet,\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_0(\mathbb P^1(K),\mathbb Z)=\operatorname{St}(K^2)$.

Homological consequence of Serre's theorem

The stable homological complex
\[C_\bullet(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma\text{-st}}_\bullet,\mathbb Z) :=\operatorname{coker}\bigl(C_\bullet(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma\text{-un}}_\bullet,\mathbb Z) \longrightarrow C_\bullet(\mathcal{BT}_\bullet,\mathbb Z)\bigr).\]

Since $|\mathcal{BT}_\bullet|$ is contractible, the long exact sequence gives

\[H_1(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma\text{-st}}_\bullet,\mathbb Z) \cong \widetilde H_0(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma\text{-un}}_\bullet,\mathbb Z) \cong \operatorname{St}(K^2).\]

The left-hand side is a finitely generated, free $\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$-module; hence so is $\operatorname{St}(K^2)$.

Yet another homotopy equivalence

By adapting the proof strategy of Grayson–Quillen, we are able to obtain the following generalization of Serre's result from rank 2:

Theorem 1 (Boeckle–V. ’26)
Let $I\subset A$ be a nonzero proper ideal and let $\Gamma(I)\subset\mathrm{GL}_r(K)$ be the corresponding principal congruence subgroup. Then there is a $\mathrm{GL}_r(A)$-equivariant homotopy equivalence \[ \left|\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma(I)\text{-}un}_\bullet\right| \simeq \left|\mathcal{T}_\bullet(K^r)\right|. \]

Corollary to Theorem 1

Stable homological complex
As in rank 2, define \[ C_\bullet\bigl(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma(I)\text{-}st}_\bullet,\mathbb Z\bigr) := \operatorname{coker}\Bigl( C_\bullet\bigl(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma(I)\text{-}un}_\bullet,\mathbb Z\bigr) \longrightarrow C_\bullet\bigl(\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_\bullet,\mathbb Z\bigr) \Bigr). \]
Corollary
The $(r-1)$-st homology \[ \mathrm{St}^{\Gamma(I)} :=H_{r-1}\bigl(\mathcal{BT}^{\Gamma(I)\text{-}st}_\bullet,\mathbb Z\bigr) \xrightarrow[\sim]{\mathrm{GQ}_{\Gamma(I)}} \operatorname{St}(K^r) \] is isomorphic to the Steinberg module as a $\mathrm{GL}_r(A)$-module.

Remarks on Theorem 1

Generalization to function fields of curves
The result of Theorem 1 holds more generally for the function field $K=\mathbb F_q(C)$ of a smooth, projective, geometrically connected curve $C$ over $\mathbb F_q$, after fixing a closed point $\infty\in C$.
Work in progress
We also expect the result to hold for $p'$-torsion-free congruence subgroups. This extension is work in progress.

Harmonic cocycles

Let $\widehat{\mathcal{BT}}_{r-1}$ be the set of pointed chambers $(C,v)$, where $C\in\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_{r-1}$ and $v\in C$.

Definition
Let $V$ be an abelian group. A map $f:\widehat{\mathcal{BT}}_{r-1}\to V$ is a harmonic cocycle if:

Alternating: $f(C,v)=\operatorname{sgn}(C,v,v')\,f(C,v')$.

Harmonic: for every panel $e\in\mathcal{BT}^{(r)}_{r-2}$, \[\sum_{\substack{(C,v)\in\widehat{\mathcal{BT}}_{r-1}\\v\in e}}f(C,v)=0.\]

Write $C_{har}(V)$ for the group of such cocycles. It carries a natural $\mathrm{GL}_r(K)$-action.

If $V$ is a $\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$-module, the resulting $\Gamma$-action on $C_{har}(V)$ is also defined.

Teitelbaum's theorem ($r=2$)

Teitelbaum
Let $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$ be a $p'$-torsion-free congruence subgroup and let $V$ be a $\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$-module. Restriction to $\Gamma$-stable edges gives an isomorphism, followed by Serre's isomorphism:
\[C_{har}(V)^\Gamma \xrightarrow[\sim]{\mathrm{Te}_\Gamma} \mathrm{St}^\Gamma\underset{\Gamma}{\otimes}V \xrightarrow[\sim]{\mathrm{Serre}} \mathrm{St}(K^2)\underset{\Gamma}{\otimes}V.\]
Explicitly: \[f\longmapsto \sum_{e\in\Gamma\backslash\widehat{\mathcal{BT}}^{\Gamma\text{-st}}_1/\{\pm1\}} [\overrightarrow e]\otimes f(\overrightarrow e).\]

Thus a $\Gamma$-invariant harmonic cocycle is determined by, and can be reconstructed from, its values on $\Gamma$-stable edges.

Sketch of proof of Theorem 1

Grayson's Lemma 1.9. Let $X_\bullet$ be a simplicial complex, $T_\bullet$ a poset, and for each $\sigma\in T_0$ let $X^\sigma_\bullet\subseteq X_\bullet$ be a subcomplex satisfying:
(i) $\sigma\leq\tau\implies X^\sigma_\bullet\supseteq X^\tau_\bullet$; (ii) $X_\bullet=\bigcup_{\sigma\in T_0}X^\sigma_\bullet$; (iii) each $X^\sigma_\bullet$ is contractible; (iv) $T_\gamma=\{\sigma\in T_0:\gamma\in X^\sigma_\bullet\}$ is contractible for every $\gamma\in X_\bullet$.
Conclusion: $|X_\bullet|\simeq |T_\bullet|$ canonically.
$X_\bullet$ $\simeq\;\longrightarrow$ $X^{(1)}_\bullet$ $T_\bullet$
$Z_\bullet$ $Z_\bullet\subseteq X^{(1)}_\bullet\times T_\bullet,\quad (s,\sigma)\in Z_0\iff s\in X^\sigma_\bullet$
$\overset{p_1}{\swarrow}$ $\overset{p_2}{\searrow}$
$p_1$ is a homotopy equivalence by (iv). $p_2$ is a homotopy equivalence by (iii).
Fiber lemma. Let $X,Y$ be posets and $Z\subseteq X\times Y$ a closed subposet. If each fiber $Z_x=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in Z\}$ (respectively, $Z_y=\{x\in X:(x,y)\in Z\}$) is contractible, then $p_1:Z\to X$ (respectively, $p_2:Z\to Y$) is a homotopy equivalence. This follows from Quillen's Theorem A.
